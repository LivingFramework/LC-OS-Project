# LC-OS Adoption Levels

**Purpose:** Help users choose the lightest amount of governance needed for their project.

**Last updated:** 2026-07-13

---

## Why levels?

Modern AI tools are stronger than the tools LC-OS was originally built around. Many now offer project spaces, uploaded files, longer context windows, saved memory, connectors, and agentic workflows.

That means LC-OS should not be adopted as an all-or-nothing system.

Use the smallest level that protects the work.

---

## Level 0 — Native AI Workspace Only

**Use when:**

- the task is short
- mistakes are low-cost
- there are few or no numbers
- you do not need auditability
- the AI tool's built-in project or memory features are enough

**Typical setup:**

- one AI project/chat/workspace
- uploaded reference files if needed
- no formal LC-OS documents

**Example:** drafting a short email sequence, brainstorming names, outlining a small document.

---

## Level 1 — Light LC-OS

**Use when:**

- the work spans multiple sessions
- decisions need to persist
- you want less re-explaining
- the project has a clear objective but low operational risk

**Use:**

- Running Document
- optional Canonical Numbers if numbers matter

**Example:** planning a small launch, preparing a proposal, writing a long article over several sessions.

---

## Level 2 — Governed LC-OS

**Use when:**

- accuracy matters
- numbers, citations, decisions, or assumptions must remain stable
- the project has several moving parts
- previous AI errors have caused rework

**Use:**

- Running Document
- Canonical Numbers
- Failure Log when failures recur
- lightweight Repair Protocol
- occasional Stability Ping

**Example:** research drafting, budget planning, client onboarding, financial analysis, product strategy.

---

## Level 3 — Full LC-OS

**Use when:**

- work spans many weeks or months
- there are multiple domains or pillars
- decisions have long-term consequences
- trust, tone, or boundary issues matter
- you need structured challenge and disagreement

**Use:**

- Strategy Master
- Running Document
- Canonical Numbers
- Failure Log
- Repair Protocol
- Stability Ping
- Challenge Protocol
- Affective Governance when relevant

**Example:** sustained research programme, multi-domain personal operating system, long-term business or client strategy.

---

## Level 4 — Research / Audit Mode

**Use when:**

- work must be reconstructable later
- failures need detailed traceability
- the collaboration itself is being studied
- you need a formal record for review, publication, or governance

**Use:**

- full Level 3 stack
- TraceSpec
- explicit versioning
- regular reviews
- preserved logs and artefact history

**Example:** longitudinal research, high-stakes advisory work, audited AI-assisted workflows.

---

## Decision rule

If you are unsure, start one level lower.

Add structure only after one of these appears:

- repeated context loss
- numerical inconsistency
- disagreement about what was decided
- hidden or repeated failure
- drift across tools or files
- loss of trust in the collaboration

LC-OS should reduce friction. If the system feels heavier than the problem, simplify it.
